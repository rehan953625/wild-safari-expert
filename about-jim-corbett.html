India's First National Park (Est. 1936)

A Natural Heritage Spanning Nearly a Century

Created on 8 August 1936 as Hailey National Park, this protected foothills landscape is celebrated as India's first national park and the oldest national park on mainland Asia. Briefly known as Ramganga National Park in the mid-1950s, it was renamed Jim Corbett National Park by 1957 in memory of Edward James (Jim) Corbett—the Kumaon-born naturalist, author, and early advocate for wildlife observation and forest preservation.

Born in Nainital in 1875, Jim Corbett spent decades walking the valleys of Kumaon and Garhwal, later classic books on Himalayan village life and forest ecology while championing camera photography and nature protection. On 1 April 1973, this park was chosen as the inaugural launch site for India's landmark Project Tiger conservation initiative.

🐅 Thriving Sub-Himalayan Habitat The broader reserve spans 1,288.31 sq. km of interconnected Sal woodland, river valleys, and grasslands supporting Royal Bengal tigers and Asian elephants.
🌊 Lifelines of Ramganga & Kosi Fed by the Ramganga, Sonanadi, Mandal, Palain, and Kosi rivers, creating rich wetland corridors for waterbirds, gharials, and golden mahseer.
Location, Terrain & Rivers

Geography of the Jim Corbett Landscape

Set where the outer Himalayan hills meet the northern plains, Corbett brings together wooded ridges, pebble stream beds, river valleys, and open meadows in a single connected landscape.

📍 Foothills Setting & Gateway Town

Corbett lies in the Kumaon and Garhwal foothills of Uttarakhand, spanning parts of Nainital, Pauri Garhwal, and adjoining Almora forest belts. The market town of Ramnagar serves as the main rail and highway hub for visitors.

The broader reserve covers 1,288.31 sq. km—comprising 821.99 sq. km of core woodland and meadow habitat along with 466.32 sq. km of adjoining buffer forest including the Sonanadi sanctuary belt.

⛰️ Shivalik Ridges, Bhabar & Terai

The terrain rises from gentle plains into the undulating Shivalik hills, narrow ravines, and broad valley floors. Along the southern Bhabar belt, porous gravel and boulder soils absorb seasonal mountain streams underground.

Farther downslope toward the Terai belt, finer moisture-rich soils bring water back to the surface, supporting lush marshlands, tall grasses, and diverse woodland flora.

💧 Ramganga Reservoir & River Valleys

The Ramganga River winds through the heart of the reserve and opens into the wide Kalagarh reservoir across the Dhikala valley, joined by tributaries such as the Sonanadi, Palain, and Mandal.

Along the eastern edge near Dhikuli and Ramnagar, the boulder-strewn Kosi River forms a scenic corridor for winter birdwatching and riverside resort stays.

🌤️ Seasonal Shifts Across the Valley

Corbett experiences three distinct seasons: crisp, misty winters (November to February), warm, sunlit summers (March to June) when animals frequently visit perennial river pools, and lush monsoon rains (July to September).

Because wildlife moves naturally with changing water and foliage conditions, every season offers its own distinct scenery and birding highlights.

Woodlands, Meadows & Biodiversity

Natural Habitats & Wildlife of Corbett

A mosaic of tall Sal groves, open savannah grasslands, riverbanks, and Shivalik slopes supports one of northern India's richest webs of forest life.

🌳 Sal & Mixed Deciduous Groves

Sal (Shorea robusta) forms the signature canopy across much of the reserve, mingled with haldu, rohini, jamun, amla, bamboo, and bright red flame-of-the-forest blossoms in spring.

🌾 Open Chaurs (Grasslands)

Flat valley clearings known locally as chaurs—most famously Dhikala Chaur and Bijrani meadows—provide rich grazing for spotted deer, sambar, hog deer, and elephant herds.

🐅 Mammals of the Foothills

Alongside the Royal Bengal Tiger and Asian Elephant, the forest shelters leopards, sloth bears, Himalayan black bears, jackals, barking deer (kakar), chital, sambar, and river otters.

🦅 600+ Bird & Aquatic Species

A paradise for birdwatchers featuring great pied hornbills, Pallas's fish eagles, crested serpent eagles, tawny fish owls, kalij pheasants, kingfishers, gharials, and golden mahseer.

📖 Remembering Edward James (Jim) Corbett (1875–1955)

Born in Nainital, Edward James Corbett grew up exploring the trails, rivers, and villages of the Kumaon hills. Through his widely read books—including Man-Eaters of Kumaon, My India, and Jungle Lore—he introduced readers around the world to the natural beauty and people of Uttarakhand.

In his later years, Corbett became a passionate pioneer of wildlife cinema photography and forest preservation, advocating that India's wild habitats and big cats be protected for future generations. Naming the park in his honour in 1957 celebrated his lifelong bond with the Kumaon foothills.

Key Milestones

Timeline of Jim Corbett National Park

How a scenic valley in the Kumaon foothills evolved into one of Asia's most celebrated wildlife landscapes.

🕰️ Early 1900s

  • Conservationists and forestry naturalists in Kumaon proposed setting aside the Ramganga valley and Patli Dun forests as a dedicated nature sanctuary.

🌿 8 August 1936

  • Established as Hailey National Park covering 323.75 sq. km—becoming the first national park in India and on mainland Asia.

🏞️ 1954 – 1957

  • Briefly named Ramganga National Park after the river running through its valley, and renamed Jim Corbett National Park in 1957 in memory of Jim Corbett.

🐅 1 April 1973

  • Selected as the inaugural launch site for India's Project Tiger, focusing on holistic habitat and prey-base preservation.

🌲 1991 Expansion

  • Adjoining woodland belts and the Sonanadi Wildlife Sanctuary were linked with the reserve to strengthen elephant and wildlife movement corridors.

🗺️ Modern Landscape

  • Today, the broader 1,288.31 sq. km landscape balances core habitat protection with organized eco-tourism sectors around Ramnagar and Kotdwar.
Visitor Exploration Sectors

How to Explore the Corbett Sectors

To keep forest trails peaceful, visitor drives are organized across dedicated sectors, each offering its own scenery and access style.

Dhikala Meadow Sector

Famous for sweeping Chaurs, the Ramganga reservoir, and heritage rest houses. Day visitors explore Dhikala aboard the 16-seater open Canter coach from Dhangarhi Gate, while overnight lodge guests travel by 4x4 Gypsy.

Seasonal Planner & Forest Etiquette

Best Time to Visit & Mindful Travel Pointers

Plan your clothing, drive slots, and forest etiquette according to the season for a comfortable and respectful wildlife holiday.

❄️ Winter Months (Nov to Feb)

  • Weather: 6°C to 24°C (Misty mornings, pleasant sunny afternoons).
  • Highlights: All major sectors including Dhikala are open; peak season for migratory waterbirds and family holidays.
  • What to Pack: Warm jackets, thermals, gloves, and caps for early morning open-top drives.

☀️ Summer Months (Mar to Jun)

  • Weather: 22°C to 39°C.
  • Highlights: Clear visibility across woodland tracks as elephants, deer, and big cats gather near perennial river pools.
  • What to Pack: Breathable earth-toned cottons, sun hats, sunglasses, and drinking water.

🌧️ Monsoon Months (Jul to Oct)

  • Landscape: Lush emerald hills and flowing foothill streams.
  • Open Sectors: Inner meadow tracks rest for the rains, while southern sectors like Jhirna, Dhela, Phato, and Sitabani remain open subject to weather.
  • Vibe: Peaceful, uncrowded riverside resort stays.

✅ Helpful Do's on Your Drive

  • Cooperate with Your Guide: Follow the pointers shared by your local nature guide and driver throughout the excursion.
  • Keep the Forest Litter-Free: Carry back all water bottles and snack wrappers to keep the woodland clean.
  • Observe Quietly: Soft voices and neutral clothing help you spot more birds and mammals naturally.

🌿 Respecting Wildlife Space

  • Stay Seated Aboard Your Vehicle: Enjoy views from inside your Gypsy or Canter coach and step down only at designated rest points.
  • Natural Feeding Habits: Never offer food to monkeys, deer, or birds along the trail.
  • Natural Light Photography: Keep camera flash switched off so wildlife remains undisturbed.

📋 Quick Planning Notes

  • Every Drive is Unique: Wildlife moves freely across vast habitats; enjoy the complete forest atmosphere, tracks, and birdlife.
  • Local Coordination: Wild Safari coordinates private 4x4 Gypsies, local transfers, and resort stays; general park notices are available
  • Guide Fee Note: The local nature guide fee (~₹1,000) is paid directly by guests in cash at the entry gate.
