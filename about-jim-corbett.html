Kumaon & Garhwal Foothills

Overview of the Corbett Foothills Region

Originally established in August 1936 in the sub-Himalayan foothills of Uttarakhand, the Corbett landscape is widely known to travellers as one of India's oldest woodland and river-valley destinations. Located near the gateway market town of Ramnagar, the region attracts nature lovers, families, and wildlife photographers throughout the year.

Location, Terrain & Rivers

Geography of the Ramnagar & Corbett Landscape

Set where the outer Himalayan hills meet the northern plains, this region brings together wooded ridges, pebble stream beds, river valleys, and open meadows.

📍 Foothills Setting & Gateway Town

The landscape lies in the Kumaon and Garhwal foothills of Uttarakhand, spanning parts of Nainital and Pauri Garhwal districts. The market town of Ramnagar serves as the main rail and highway hub for visiting tourists.

Most private resorts, family hotels, and local Gypsy transfer hubs operate along the scenic Ramnagar–Dhikuli–Marchula corridor outside the forest boundary.

⛰️ Shivalik Ridges, Bhabar & Terai

The terrain rises from gentle plains into the undulating Shivalik hills, narrow ravines, and broad valley floors. Along the southern Bhabar belt, porous gravel and boulder soils absorb seasonal mountain streams.

Farther downslope toward the Terai belt, moisture-rich soils bring water back to the surface, supporting lush grasslands and diverse woodland flora.

💧 Ramganga & Kosi River Valleys

The Ramganga River winds through the inner valley, joined by tributaries such as the Sonanadi, Palain, and Mandal across the western and northern ridges.

Along the eastern highway belt near Dhikuli and Ramnagar, the boulder-strewn Kosi River forms a popular corridor for winter birdwatching, riverside walks, and resort stays.

🌤️ Seasonal Shifts Across the Valley

The region experiences three distinct travel seasons: crisp, misty winters (November to February), warm, sunlit summers (March to June), and lush monsoon rains (July to September).

Because weather and road access shift with the calendar, choosing the right month helps travellers pack appropriate clothing and select suitable local excursion routes.

Woodlands, Meadows & Biodiversity

Natural Habitats & Wildlife of the Region

A mosaic of tall Sal groves, open savannah grasslands, riverbanks, and Shivalik slopes supports a rich variety of sub-Himalayan forest life.

🌳 Sal & Mixed Deciduous Groves

Sal (Shorea robusta) forms the signature canopy across much of the foothills, mingled with haldu, rohini, jamun, amla, bamboo, and bright red palash blossoms in spring.

🌾 Open Chaurs (Grasslands)

Flat valley clearings known locally as chaurs provide open grazing meadows for spotted deer, sambar, hog deer, and wild elephant herds.

🐅 Mammals of the Foothills

The broader forest belt shelters Royal Bengal tigers, Asian elephants, leopards, sloth bears, jackals, barking deer (kakar), chital, sambar, and river otters.

🦅 Foothill Bird & River Life

A rewarding destination for birdwatchers featuring hornbills, fish eagles, serpent eagles, owls, kalij pheasants, kingfishers, and river life along the Kosi and Ramganga.

📖 Historical Note on the Name of the Region

Travellers often ask how the national park got its name. Originally created in 1936 as Hailey National Park and briefly called Ramganga National Park in the 1950s, the park was renamed in 1957 in historical memory of the late Kumaon-born author and naturalist Edward James Corbett (1875–1955).

  • Located about 14 km from Ramnagar along the Kosi River on a scenic rock pinnacle, visited by families and travellers year-round.

  • A peaceful stretch near Ramnagar ideal for winter birdwatching, scenic drives through Sal groves, and quiet family outings.

  • A scenic mountain drive north of Dhikuli along winding Shivalik hills and suspension bridges overlooking the river valley.

Seasonal Planner & Travel Etiquette

Best Time to Visit & Mindful Travel Pointers

Plan your clothing, drive shifts, and travel schedule according to the season for a comfortable holiday in Uttarakhand.

❄️ Winter Months (Nov to Feb)

  • Weather: 6°C to 24°C (Misty mornings, pleasant sunny afternoons).
  • Highlights: Peak season for migratory waterbirds, winter sunshine, and family holidays.
  • What to Pack: Heavy jackets, thermals, gloves, and caps for early morning open-top Gypsy drives.

☀️ Summer Months (Mar to Jun)

  • Weather: 22°C to 39°C.
  • Highlights: Clear visibility across woodland tracks as wildlife gathers near perennial river pools and waterholes.
  • What to Pack: Breathable earth-toned cotton clothes, sun hats, sunglasses, and drinking water.

🌧️ Monsoon Months (Jul to Oct)

  • Landscape: Lush green hills and flowing foothill streams.
  • Accessible Routes: Southern routes like Jhirna, Dhela, Phato, and Sitabani remain accessible subject to daily weather conditions.
  • Vibe: Peaceful, uncrowded riverside resort stays in Dhikuli.

✅ Helpful Do's on Your Drive

  • Be Ready Early: Our private Gypsy arrives at your Ramnagar or Dhikuli resort 30–45 minutes prior to departure.
  • Keep the Forest Litter-Free: Carry back all water bottles and wrappers to keep the woodland clean.
  • Observe Quietly: Soft voices and neutral clothing help you spot more birds and mammals naturally.

🌿 Respecting Nature

  • Stay Seated Aboard Your Vehicle: Enjoy views from inside your Gypsy or Canter coach and step down only at designated rest points.
  • Do Not Feed Wildlife: Never offer food to monkeys, deer, or birds along the road.
  • Natural Light Photography: Keep camera flash switched off so animals remain undisturbed.
